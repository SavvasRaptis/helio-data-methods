# Model Interpretability

## Learning objectives

By the end of this chapter, readers should be able to:

- distinguish global from local explanations;
- match an interpretation method to a scientific question;
- test explanation stability and sensitivity;
- state what an explanation does and does not establish.

## Chapter roadmap

The chapter will begin with transparent baselines and model inspection, then
introduce permutation-based and attribution-based approaches with explicit
assumptions and validation checks.

## Planned demonstrations

Examples will compare explanations across models, seeds, and correlated
features while holding the prediction task and evaluation data fixed.

## Planned exercises

Readers will identify misleading explanation workflows and design checks for
robustness, leakage, and dependence on the background sample.

## Common mistakes to examine

- interpreting association as causation;
- presenting one explanation without a stability check;
- ignoring correlated or redundant inputs;
- explaining a poorly evaluated model.

## Summary and references

To be developed with the applied and research case studies.
